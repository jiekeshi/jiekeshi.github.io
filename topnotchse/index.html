Influential Software Engineering Papers

Note: This webpage is the software-engineering counterpart of TopNotch by Konrad Rieck, which ranked security papers until September 2025, when Google Scholar began blocking automated crawling. Citation counts here are primarily taken from Semantic Scholar and cross-checked against OpenAlex. Only when the two sources disagree strongly is Google Scholar consulted for the final verdict — sparingly, through a rate-limited search API, as Scholar can no longer be crawled at scale. Each entry names the source of its count. Be aware that Scholar counts everything it can find (preprints, theses, reports) and therefore runs systematically higher than the other two sources; papers whose count comes from Google Scholar may thus look somewhat inflated next to their Semantic Scholar-sourced neighbors.

This webpage is an attempt to assemble a ranking of top-cited papers from the area of software engineering. The ranking is automatically created based on citations of papers published at top software engineering venues. Conferences and journals are ranked separately. In particular, the ranking is based on the four tier-1 conferences

  • International Conference on Software Engineering (ICSE)
  • ACM International Conference on the Foundations of Software Engineering (FSE)
  • IEEE/ACM International Conference on Automated Software Engineering (ASE)
  • ACM SIGSOFT International Symposium on Software Testing and Analysis (ISSTA)

the following tier-2 conferences

  • IEEE/ACM International Conference on Mining Software Repositories (MSR)
  • IEEE International Conference on Software Analysis, Evolution and Reengineering (SANER)
  • IEEE International Conference on Software Maintenance and Evolution (ICSME)
  • IEEE International Symposium on Software Reliability Engineering (ISSRE)
  • IEEE International Conference on Software Testing, Verification and Validation (ICST)
  • IEEE/ACM International Conference on Program Comprehension (ICPC)

and the following journals

  • IEEE Transactions on Software Engineering (TSE)
  • ACM Transactions on Software Engineering and Methodology (TOSEM)
  • Empirical Software Engineering (EMSE)

The paper lists are determined by crawling the DBLP service; citations come from Semantic Scholar, OpenAlex, and Google Scholar. As these services limit crawling activity, the update interval for the ranking is large, such that citation counts change on average every two months.

Top of the Notch

Absolute citations are not necessarily a good indicator for the impact of a paper, as the number of citations usually grows with the age of a paper. The primary ranking therefore normalizes the citations of each paper by its age, comparing it against the average of its publication year.

Limitations

As with any ranking, the presented results do not necessarily reflect the true impact of a paper. Citations are only one metric to assess the reception of a paper and are insufficient to characterize all aspects contributing to the relevance of scientific work. Moreover, the underlying data may contain errors or missing information. Errare humanum est.
